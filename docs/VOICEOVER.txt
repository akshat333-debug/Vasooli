VASOOLI - VOICEOVER SCRIPT FOR TEXT-TO-SPEECH
=============================================

Generate FOUR separate audio files, one per segment. Paste only the text
between the "BEGIN" and "END" markers. Do not paste the headers, the notes,
or these instructions - they are not spoken.

Numbers are already written the way they should be read aloud. Do not
"correct" them back into digits or symbols; a rupee sign or a slash will be
read wrong by most generators.

Recommended settings: measured, even-paced voice. Speed zero point nine five
to one point zero. Nothing enthusiastic - the argument here works because the
numbers are unflattering, and an excited read fights it.

Target: about four minutes forty seconds total.


=============================================
SEGMENT 1 - TERMINAL  (target about 2:00)
=============================================
Visual: six commands run live in the terminal.

--- BEGIN SEGMENT 1 ---

A recurring debit fails on Razorpay. It auto-retries three times, and then the
subscription halts. It stops charging itself, and it comes back only if the
customer goes and updates their card.

One hundred synthetic at-risk subscriptions, seeded and reproducible. Dead
mandates, exhausted budgets, amounts above the R B I cap. Hard cases on
purpose.

Two arms over the identical batch, same random draws. The baseline is the
fixed schedule dunning tools ship. Vasooli diagnoses the failure first, then
decides.

Look at the top row, and notice that it is boring. About fifty-seven and a
half thousand rupees against about fifty-nine thousand. Both arms recover
roughly the same money this cycle. If that were my claim, I would not have a
project.

Here is the claim. The baseline spent one hundred and sixty attempts to get
that. Vasooli spent seventy-seven. The budget is three deep and it does not
refill, so what those extra eighty-three attempts bought the baseline is five
more halted subscriptions. Twenty-seven, against twenty-two.

Halted is not deleted. But it stops charging automatically, the invoices it
accrues are never charged on their own, and getting it back depends on a
disengaged person choosing to act. That is thirteen thousand rupees a month
moved off autopilot. Ten times the one-cycle difference, in month one, and it
recurs. A dunning dashboard reports the top row. The top row is the wrong row.

The full rule trace for one record. All seven stopping rules, in order, with
why each passed or fired. Run it again and it is identical, which is what
having no model in the decision path buys you.

Then, forty independent seeds. On rupees per attempt it wins forty out of
forty. On raw rupees collected, budget ignored, it wins twenty-five and loses
fourteen. The tool prints that itself, because you would find it anyway.

It prints this too. The scheduler is over-confident by fourteen points in its
top confidence bucket. It is most wrong exactly where it is most sure, which
is the worst place to be wrong.

Then I tried to break my own thesis, and decomposed the gain. Refusing doomed
attempts is eighty-two per cent of it. Timing is eighteen. The most elaborate
part of this engine is the smaller half.

This is the money-side circuit breaker tripping, on camera.

And this is a real Razorpay test-mode API call. An actual subscription, not a
mock. It stops right there, because activation needs the customer to
authenticate the mandate, and a batch job should not do that on their behalf.

--- END SEGMENT 1 ---


=============================================
SEGMENT 2 - BROWSER  (target about 1:30)
=============================================
Visual: the deployed site. Home, escalation queue, Rulebook, Ledger tamper
demo, Records.

--- BEGIN SEGMENT 2 ---

Every one of the three hundred retries this batch was allowed to spend, one
square per attempt. The baseline fills what is left. Vasooli leaves most of it
unspent.

Refusing to debit is only half an answer, because the rupee is still owed. So
every record the engine declines carries a route, as structured data on the
decision. Seventy-three thousand rupees goes to a customer-present payment
link with additional factor authentication, because the answer to "above the
cap" is not "give up". The baseline produces zero escalations.

All seven stopping rules, each with the legal basis it rests on and how many
subscriptions it stopped in this batch.

And every decision is hash-chained before it is acted on. That is not a claim
you have to take on trust. Tamper with a row yourself, and watch the verifier
locate exactly where the chain breaks.

--- END SEGMENT 2 ---


=============================================
SEGMENT 3 - CODE  (target about 0:25)
=============================================
Visual: the decide module open in the editor, scrolled to the top. Do not
scroll during this segment.

--- BEGIN SEGMENT 3 ---

Diagnosis uses a language model to read free-text bank errors, which is a
genuine language problem. But no model runs in this file. Seven stopping rules
and a deterministic scheduler decide whether to move money. A model reads what
the bank wrote. It never decides whether to debit anyone.

--- END SEGMENT 3 ---


=============================================
SEGMENT 4 - CLOSE  (target about 0:45)
=============================================
Visual: pytest running in the terminal, ending on "229 passed".

--- BEGIN SEGMENT 4 ---

Two hundred and twenty-nine tests, hermetic. No network, no A P I key.
Ninety-two per cent coverage.

Twenty-seven logged defects, five found by an external reviewer who caught
what I could not see in my own code. The most recent: I had been calling a
halted subscription permanent, and it is not. Razorpay documents the way back.
Correcting it made the claim smaller and made it true, and I would rather hand
you the smaller claim.

Retries are a budget of three. Vasooli spends them on the failures that can
actually be recovered, refuses the ones that cannot, loudly and on the record,
and gives every rupee it refuses somewhere to go. Same money this cycle, half
the budget, and thirteen thousand rupees a month still collecting on its own.

--- END SEGMENT 4 ---
